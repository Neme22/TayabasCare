Objectives of the study

The overarching objective of this study is to design, develop, and evaluate TayabasCare, a predictive and data-driven inspection support system that utilizes historical inspection records to forecast possible sanitary violations among business establishments within Tayabas City. The study aims to transform the traditional, manual, and often inconsistent inspection workflow of the City Health Office into a more efficient, proactive, and evidence-based process. Through the integration of predictive analytics, machine learning, digital reporting, and interactive data visualization, the study seeks not only to modernize local health compliance monitoring but also to strengthen public health safety and regulatory enforcement in the city. More specifically, the study intends to accomplish the following detailed objectives:
1.To gather, consolidate, and digitize historical inspection data from the City Health Office of Tayabas City into a structured database.
The system will collect inspection records including establishment information, violation history, inspection dates, and compliance status from the City Health Office's existing records. By organizing scattered paper-based or manually-kept records into a centralized digital database, the system creates a standardized repository where all establishment profiles, past violations, and inspection histories are stored systematically. This digitization replaces manual filing systems with an organized digital archive that allows quick retrieval of establishment information and provides the foundation for analyzing violation patterns across different establishment types and time periods.

2.To analyze historical inspection data to identify violation patterns and establish risk scoring criteria based on establishment characteristics and compliance history.
The system will examine digitized inspection records to identify patterns such as which types of establishments frequently violate regulations, which violations occur most commonly, and whether certain establishment characteristics correlate with higher non-compliance rates. Through statistical analysis of violation frequency, recurrence rates, and establishment attributes, the system establishes risk scoring criteria using measurable factors such as: number of previous violations, time since last violation, establishment category, compliance history percentage, and follow-up action completion rates. This analysis provides the basis for creating a simple but effective risk assessment formula that categorizes establishments without requiring complex machine learning algorithms.

3.To design and implement TayabasCare as a web-based platform that manages inspection records, calculates risk scores, and supports inspection scheduling and documentation.
The system will provide a centralized web interface where health inspectors and administrators can access establishment profiles, record new inspection results using digital forms, view automatically calculated risk scores based on the established scoring criteria, and schedule upcoming inspections prioritized by risk levels. The platform includes modules for encoding inspection findings through structured checklists, uploading supporting documents or photos, generating inspection reports, and maintaining complete audit trails of all activities. Role-based access controls ensure inspectors can perform field data entry while administrators have additional privileges for managing users, reviewing analytics, and adjusting system parameters. This web-based implementation eliminates paper-based workflows, centralizes fragmented records, and provides the City Health Office with organized digital tools for managing their inspection operations.

4.To implement automated risk assessment and notification features that prioritize high-risk establishments and alert personnel about inspection schedules and compliance issues.
The system will automatically calculate risk scores for each establishment using the predefined scoring formula based on factors like violation history, establishment type, and compliance trends, then classify establishments into risk categories (low, moderate, high) to help inspectors prioritize their workload. Automated notifications alert inspectors when inspections are due based on risk levels—high-risk establishments receive more frequent inspection schedules while low-risk establishments have longer intervals between visits. The system sends email reminders to inspectors about upcoming scheduled inspections, alerts administrators when establishments accumulate violations exceeding threshold limits, and notifies relevant personnel when follow-up actions remain incomplete past deadline dates. This automated prioritization ensures limited inspection resources focus on establishments most likely to present compliance concerns based on their documented history.

5.To provide analytical dashboards and reporting features that visualize violation trends, compliance statistics, and establishment risk distributions for informed decision-making.
The system will generate visual analytics through dashboards displaying key metrics such as total inspections conducted per month, violation rates by establishment category, distribution of risk levels across all registered establishments, most common violation types, and compliance improvement trends over time. Interactive charts and graphs allow administrators to filter data by date ranges, establishment types, geographic areas, or specific violation categories to gain insights into sanitation patterns across Tayabas City. The reporting module produces exportable summaries in PDF or Excel formats suitable for presentation to City Health Office leadership or inclusion in official reports, showing statistical breakdowns of inspection activities, establishment compliance performance rankings, and inspector productivity metrics. These analytical tools transform raw inspection data into actionable insights that help administrators identify which establishment categories require targeted interventions, which areas of the city have higher concentration of violations, and whether overall compliance is improving or declining over time, supporting evidence-based policy decisions and strategic resource allocation without relying solely on manual observations or anecdotal assessments.
